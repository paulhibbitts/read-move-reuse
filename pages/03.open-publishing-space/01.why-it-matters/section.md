Hello there, I'm Paul Hibbitts – after 25 years teaching user interface design, mostly at SFU, I now build free, open-source tools that turn Markdown into websites for open educators and publishers.

**Open for reuse:** open textbooks and guides are meant to be reused and remixed, but they often live inside a single platform – harder to adapt, move or self-host elsewhere. What if every page stayed portable?

[definition title="The DIY answer: plain Markdown"]
A simple text format for web pages – `**bold**`, `## Heading`, `- list item`. It's supported almost everywhere, from GitHub and note-taking apps to Google Docs. Keep every page in Markdown, on a free site you control.
[/definition]

Best of all for reuse, anyone can get the Markdown behind any page they're viewing – no access to the source files needed. Markdown has also become the lingua franca of generative AI, for better or worse, making open content as easy for AI tools to work with as it is for people.

No slides needed: this one guide works as a presentation (← and → move between pages), a web page to read at your own pace, an LMS embed, a printout or Markdown – all from a single source. Write once, use anywhere.
