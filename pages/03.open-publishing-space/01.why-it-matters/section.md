Hello there, I'm Paul Hibbitts – after 25 years teaching user interface design, mostly at SFU, I now build free, open-source tools that turn Markdown into websites for open educators and publishers.

**Open for reuse:** open textbooks and guides are meant to be reused and remixed (the [5Rs](https://opencontent.org/definition/?target=_blank) of open content), but they often live inside a single platform – harder to adapt, move or self-host elsewhere. What if every page stayed portable?

[definition title="The DIY answer: plain Markdown"]
A simple text format for web pages – `**bold**`, `## Heading`, `- list item`. It's supported almost everywhere, from GitHub and note-taking apps to Google Docs. Keep every page on a free site you control.
[/definition]

Best of all for reuse, anyone can get the Markdown behind any page – no source files needed – with its callouts, math and diagrams intact, ready for [Docsify-This](https://docsify-this.net/?target=_blank) or GitHub. That Markdown is also widely used by generative AI tools, so open content is as easy for them to work with as it is for people.

No slides needed: this one guide is also a presentation (← and → move between pages), an LMS embed, a printout or Markdown – all from a single source. Write once, use anywhere.
