**Open both ways:** content that's open for readers often stays inside the platform for its authors – harder to edit elsewhere, move or keep. What if it were open for both?

This course shows two free tools that are designed for readers and learners, let authors move their content, and let anyone reuse it.

[definition title="The DIY answer: plain Markdown"]
A simple text format for web pages – `**bold**`, `## Heading`, `- list item`. It's supported almost everywhere, from GitHub and note-taking apps to Google Docs, and it's widely used by other tools, including AI. Keep every page in Markdown, on a free site you control.
[/definition]

**Reuse anywhere:** anyone can get the Markdown behind any page they're viewing, with its callouts, math and diagrams intact – so it opens as it is in [Docsify-This](https://docsify-this.net/?target=_blank) (which shows Markdown as web pages) or on GitHub.

**See the proof:** [a Pressbooks book](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/universaldesign2/?target=_blank) and [the same book in Open Publishing Space](https://demo.hibbittsdesign.org/grav-open-publishing-quark2-pressbooks-udl/?target=_blank), converted with no revisions – more in [From Pressbooks or Your LMS](/guide/modules/from-pressbooks-or-your-lms).

**One course, many uses:** read it at your own pace, present it like slides (← and → move between pages), embed it in an LMS, print it or get its Markdown – all from the same pages. Write once, use anywhere.
